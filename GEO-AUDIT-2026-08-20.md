## Executive Summary

**Overall GEO Score: 63/100 (Fair)**

The on-page work is close to finished; the off-page work has not started. CamFinTech's
technical and schema foundation is genuinely strong — 14 AI crawlers explicitly allowed and
three of them verified fetching live content during this audit, `llms.txt` plus
`llms-full.txt` correctly discoverable, a clean `@id`-linked entity graph with zero JSON-LD
parse errors across all 24 pages, 53 unique FAQ pairs, and an answer-first opening sentence
on every content page. That is a better foundation than most of the competitors it is
trying to be cited alongside.

What is missing is everything outside the domain. An independent search sweep found **zero**
third-party editorial mentions, **zero** directory listings, and **no Wikidata item**
(API-confirmed). Every corroborating signal about CamFinTech currently traces back to
CamFinTech. On-site, the two highest-leverage levers remain unpulled: **four outbound
citations across the entire site** — none to a primary Cambodian regulatory source — and an
entity graph that never links the firm's own three properties together.

**On the score drop:** the 63 is a measurement change, not a regression. This audit
hard-verified brand signals through the Wikidata and GitHub APIs rather than estimating them,
scoring Brand Authority at 17 against a 33–56 range in prior runs. Hold Brand at the
five-run median and the composite lands at **68 — squarely inside the established band.**
Nothing on the site got worse. One thing did: content is now 63 days stale, up from 42.

## High Priority Issues (fix within 1 week)

### H1. Four outbound citations across the entire site — none to a regulatory source

24 pages, 21,669 words, **4** outbound links to **2** domains (adb.org ×3, worldbank.org ×1).
Zero links to NBC, NBFSA, SERC, GDT or MEF.

`/products/dasp` names *Prakas* four times and the homepage three times, with no link to any
Prakas text. A firm selling regulatory fluency cites nothing regulatory.

This is the highest-leverage unpulled lever on the site. The Georgia Tech / Princeton / IIT
Delhi GEO study ranks "Cite Sources" among the top three interventions for AI visibility. It
has been flagged in three consecutive audits (as H4 on 2026-07-30) and has moved by zero.

**Fix:** 2–4 primary-source links per glossary and learn page — NBC Prakas and circulars,
SERC Prakas 093, GDT CamInvoice notifications, MEF policy documents, NBFSA registers. One
content sweep across 16 pages.

### H2. The real LinkedIn page is invisible; an unaffiliated "Camfintech" profile ranks first

[kh.linkedin.com/in/aston-hill-469944322](https://kh.linkedin.com/in/aston-hill-469944322),
titled "General Manager at Camfintech," is live and indexed, and was the **#1 result** for a
LinkedIn-scoped brand query. Per the standing guardrail this person is **not associated with
CamFinTech Cambodia**.

The severity is compounded by the second half: CamFinTech's own company page
(`/company/118224010/`) **did not surface in any search**, including a `linkedin.com`-restricted
query in which competitor Acclime Cambodia's page *did* surface. For LinkedIn-scoped queries
the unaffiliated profile is currently the **only** "Camfintech" LinkedIn signal an AI system
can find — and it will be read as a fact about the firm's staff.

Separately, the `william-mall` slug surfaces an unrelated William Mall (Ocean Foods, Virginia
Beach) and 60+ other William Malletts; the founder's profile did not appear in top results.
The slug/name mismatch is deliberate per project convention — this is not a request to change
it — but it is measurably degrading entity resolution and the company↔person edge needs to
carry the weight instead.

**Fix:** make the company page fully public and complete (logo, banner, description matching
`Organization.description`, website, HQ, industry, specialties), set a readable vanity URL,
post on a cadence so LinkedIn indexes it, and have William Mallett list CamFinTech as current
employer so the person↔company edge exists in LinkedIn's own graph. Report the impersonating
profile in parallel.

### H3. 204 icon-font ligature nodes are read as body text by AI crawlers

12 `<span className="material-symbols-outlined">` across 5 files (`app/page.tsx`,
`app/products/dasp/DaspContent.tsx`, `app/components/FAQSection.tsx`, `RelatedReading.tsx`,
`RelatedServices.tsx`). **Zero** carry `aria-hidden`. They render 204 nodes across 18 pages.

Because the ligature name is real text content, any extractor concatenates it into the heading:

| What a reader sees | What a crawler extracts |
|---|---|
| ✓ We do | `check_circleWe do` |
| ⃠ We don't | `blockWe don't` |
| ✓ In scope | `check_circleIn scope` |
| ⃠ Bright lines — out of scope | `blockBright lines — out of scope` |

Both corrupted heading pairs sit on the homepage services section and the DASP product page —
the two most commercially important blocks on the site. Every glossary and learn page also
receives `expand_more` ×3 and `article` ×3+ as stray words. Screen readers announce them too.

**Fix — minimum:** add `aria-hidden="true"` to all 12 spans (~10 min).
**Fix — better:** replace the 14 distinct icons with inline SVG. That also removes a
**310 KB render-blocking third-party font** from `fonts.googleapis.com` (full variable-axis
range `opsz,wght,FILL,GRAD@20..48,100..700,0..1,-50..200`) loaded for 14 glyphs.

### H4. Three owned properties, zero schema wiring between them

- **riel.report** — own publication, linked from 3 pages. **Zero JSON-LD**, `llms.txt` 404,
  generic robots.txt, no publisher backlink.
- **insights.camfintech.com** — **128+ archive items**, cross-submitted in robots.txt, but
  **zero inbound links from camfintech.com**, zero JSON-LD, no `llms.txt`, no AI-crawler allowlist.
- Neither appears in `Organization.sameAs`, which holds exactly two anchors (LinkedIn, GitHub).

The insights archive is the largest untapped citability asset the firm owns, and it is
orphaned from the flagship domain. A sitemap cross-submission in robots.txt is a discovery
hint, not an authority link. Three domains of genuine content are three unrelated strangers
to a crawler.

**Fix (~2 hours, best ratio in this report):** add both to `Organization.sameAs`; link
insights from the nav or `/publication`; add `Organization` + `Periodical`/`Blog` JSON-LD and
an `llms.txt` to each sibling, with `publisher` `@id` pointing at
`https://www.camfintech.com/#organization`.

### H5. The entity node AI systems resolve against has no contact details

Two schema nodes describe the same firm, and the identity is split between them:

| | `#organization` (Organization) | `#localbusiness` (ProfessionalService) |
|---|---|---|
| `sameAs` | LinkedIn + GitHub | **none** |
| `founder` | → Person | **none** |
| `knowsAbout` | 8 topics | **none** |
| `address` | **null** | full PostalAddress |
| `telephone` / `email` | **none** | +855-76-277-5645 / info@camfintech.com |
| `geo` | **none** | GeoCoordinates |

The node carrying the external identity anchors has no NAP; the node carrying NAP has no
external anchors. Neither half is a complete entity.

**Fix:** merge into one node, or populate `sameAs`/`address`/`telephone`/`email` on both and
link them with `parentOrganization` / `subOrganization`.

### H6. Twenty of 24 routes ship no `og:image`; 15 ship the wrong Twitter card

**Root cause:** in Next.js a route that declares its own `openGraph` object loses the
inherited file-convention image from `app/opengraph-image.tsx`, and a route that omits
`twitter` inherits the root layout's block verbatim.

`app/glossary/[slug]/page.tsx:24` and `app/learn/[slug]/page.tsx:24` both set `openGraph`
without `images` and never set `twitter`. All 10 glossary terms, all 6 learn articles and
`/publication` therefore publish:

```
twitter:title = "CamFinTech — Cambodia DPI Approval-Navigation & Compliance"
```

— the generic homepage card — while `og:title` is correct. Only `/`, `/glossary` and `/learn`
carry an `og:image` at all.

**Fix:** add `images: ['/opengraph-image']` to every `openGraph` block and a `twitter` block
mirroring the page's own title/description. Two files fix 16 of the 20 routes.

### H7. Nine legacy URLs redirect to the homepage that now have a real topical destination

`next.config.js` declares 28 redirects from the content-tree decommission; **20 point at `/`**.
That was a defensible call at the time — the comment reads *"no good 1:1 topical replacement
under DPI positioning."* Two things have changed since.

First, those URLs still earn: `/learn/fintech-license-cambodia` was found **still ranking in

## Medium Priority Issues (fix within 1 month)

### M1. Every substantive page is 63 days stale
All 16 content pages carry `dateModified: 2026-06-18`; sitemap `lastmod` agrees. Prakas 093
has been in force since 2025-12-30 and nothing has been revised since June. Freshness is a
live GEO input. **Regressed** from 42 days at the last audit.

### M2. The 404 page still emits two conflicting robots directives
`/404` returns both `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` and
`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`. Flagged as H1 on 2026-07-30. One-line fix.

### M3. `Article` nodes carry no `image`
All 6 learn articles. Required for Google's Article rich result and a strong extraction hint
for AI systems. Lands free alongside H6.

### M4. Only 1 of 11 service vectors has `Service` schema
SRV-11 (DASP) has it. SRV-01 through SRV-10 are described in prose and in `llms-full.txt` but
carry no markup. Add an `OfferCatalog` of `Service` nodes on the homepage, and `Course` schema
for SRV-10 (professional training).

### M5. Three hub pages are the thinnest on the site
`/learn` 244 words, `/glossary` 316, `/publication` 412. These are the entry points to the
strongest content on the domain and the least citable pages on it. Add a 150–250 word
answer-first orientation block to each.

### M6. The `Person` node is thin for a firm whose moat is the founder
`name`, `jobTitle`, `worksFor`, `sameAs` only — no `description`, `knowsAbout`, `alumniOf` or
`hasCredential`, despite `/about` carrying real biographical content that could populate all four.

### M7. Long-form articles have no sub-section granularity
All 6 learn articles: 11 `h2`, **0** `h3`, at 1,343–1,422 words. Flat structure gives
extractors fewer clean passage boundaries.

### M8. "Riel Report" has weak lexical distinctiveness and no schema to compensate
Searching the brand returns Cambodia Investment Review and Khmer Times pieces about the riel
*currency* above the publication itself. With zero JSON-LD on riel.report, nothing tells a
crawler this is a named publication rather than a phrase about money. Fixed by H4.

## Category deep dives

### AI Citability — 75/100

**Working.** 53 FAQPage Q&A pairs across 18 pages, **all 53 questions unique**, answers 36–87
words (median 57) — squarely in the range AI systems lift verbatim. Every glossary and learn
page opens with a self-contained definitional sentence that answers the title question before

**Holding it back.** Four citations site-wide (H1). 204 contaminating ligature nodes (H3).

which is disproportionately cited by AI systems. And no original data published — notable
given the firm runs a five-sensor monitoring network whose outputs would be genuinely novel,
citable primary material.

### Content E-E-A-T — 76/100

**Working.** This is the site's quiet strength. The "Honesty notes — corrections we've made"
section on both the homepage and the DASP page is a rare and genuine trustworthiness signal;
very few consulting sites publish their own corrections. Explicit bright-lines — never hold
client funds, never operate a rail, never file on the client's behalf — demonstrate
regulatory literacy more convincingly than a claims list would. Indicative pricing is
published (US$8k–25k, US$45k–75k, US$2k–8k/mo). Named author with `Person` schema, real
headshot, LinkedIn `sameAs`. Privacy and Terms carry proper `PrivacyPolicy` / `TermsOfService`

**Holding it back.** 63-day content freeze (M1). Zero primary-source citations, which reads as
a specific weakness for a regulatory practice (H1). No client evidence — no case studies,
testimonials or named engagements. That is honest at pre-revenue and should not be faked, but
it is a real ceiling. Thin `Person` node (M6). Single author across all content.

### Technical GEO — 84/100

**Working.** 14 named AI crawlers explicitly allowed, three verified fetching live content

**Holding it back.** Missing `og:image` on 20 routes and wrong Twitter cards on 15 (H6).

## Opportunities worth considering (not defects)

**The Khmer edition is an uncontested position.** The site is honest about the Khmer edition
being pending, and correctly does not claim `hreflang` it cannot serve. But Khmer-language AI
queries about Bakong, CamInvoice and DASP licensing currently return nothing from this domain,
and the competitive field in Khmer is close to empty — every competitor listed above publishes
in English only. Translating the 10 glossary terms alone would be a small, defensible first
